Day 3: Core Economic Reasoning

Every executive choice maps back to three pillars:

  • Opportunity Cost: The implicit valuation of rejected paths.
  • Incremental Concept: Analyzing the broad cost/revenue variations of structural decisions.
  • Marginal Analysis: Finding the optimal point where MR = MC.

Days 11–12: Short-Run Production

  • Day 11: Production Function with One Variable Input

    Identifying operational capacity thresholds and avoiding labor-saturation inefficiencies.

  • Day 13: Isoquants & Isocosts

Days 15–16: Cost Architecture

Day 17: Long-Run Optimization

The Evolution of Corporate Capacity:

  • The Envelope Curve: Understanding the long-run average cost profile as an array of short-run configurations.
  • Economies of Scale: Efficiencies gained via volume.
  • Economies of Scope: Efficiencies achieved by cross-utilizing assets across multiple product lines.

Phase 3: Days 21–30

Market Structures & Strategic Pricing

Slide 18 / 30

Days 21–22: Perfect Competition & Monopoly

  • Day 21: Perfect Competition
    Survival strategies in highly atomized environments; calculating short-run shutdown signals.
  • Day 22: Monopoly Frameworks
    Managing long-term entry barriers, scaling barriers, and quantifying the absolute social deadweight losses of pricing dominance.
Slide 19 / 30

Days 23–24: Imperfect Markets & Oligopoly

  • Day 23: Monopolistic Competition
    Leveraging product differentiation, continuous design innovations, and non-price brand equity pillars.
  • Day 24: Oligopoly Dynamics
    Navigating highly concentrated market segments and tracking structural price rigidity through Sweezy's kinked demand model.
Slide 20 / 30

Day 25: Classic Duopoly Paradigms

Choosing the Right Competitive Battleground:

  • Cournot Output Competition: Simultaneous volume-setting decisions when asset investments lock in output capacities.
  • Bertrand Price Competition: Severe simultaneous price undercutting, highlighting the risk of margins reverting to marginal costs.
Slide 21 / 30

Day 26: Game Theory & Strategic Moves

  • Nash Equilibrium: Identifying stable multi-competitor interactions where no player has an incentive to unilaterally deviate.
  • Prisoner's Dilemma in Business: Diagnosing value-destructive price wars and structural marketing over-expenditures.
  • Sequential Games: Using entry deterrence and commitment signals to reshape market realities.
Slide 22 / 30

Day 27: Value Capture – Price Discrimination

Strategies for segmenting consumer surplus:

  • First-Degree (Personalized Pricing): Tailoring charges to matches individuals' maximum willingness to pay via predictive data.
  • Second-Degree (Menu Pricing): Leveraging volume incentives, usage metrics, and multi-tier versioning.
  • Third-Degree (Group Pricing): Splitting demographics using distinct regional or professional profile features.
Slide 23 / 30

Days 28–29: Advanced Pricing Architectures

  • Day 28: Multi-Tier Extraction Tariffs
    Deploying two-part tariffs, strategic bundle options, product tying mechanisms, and peak-load variations.
  • Day 29: Multi-Unit Transfer Pricing
    Calculating intermediate internal asset costs to optimize tax burdens and performance between international cross-subsidiaries.
Slide 24 / 30

Day 30: Interactive Competitive Simulation

Workshop Focus: Real-time Market Intervention

  • Live gaming simulation testing team responses under duopoly and oligopoly stress.
  • Core Focus: Tracking game-theoretic matrices to establish industry equilibrium.
